02 · Prioritise first, spin second

The wheel only gets what survived my judgement

A wheel is a terrible way to set priorities, and that's the point. Before anything goes on TaskSpin, it has already been through the real work: what's urgent, what matters, what can wait, and what can be dropped. The wheel only gets the tasks that are left and roughly equal, where the hard part isn't choosing but starting.

03 · The honest nudge

If you ignore the result, the wheel still did its job

The first time you open TaskSpin, it asks:

Are you going to use this to decide tasks — or are you going to spin it, ignore the result, and secretly do the one you wanted anyway? Because if that's the case, maybe the real issue is a prioritisation system, not a randomiser.

Spinning and then reaching for a different task isn't failure. Your reaction just told you what your real priority was.

04 · Small on purpose

Scope is a product decision too

  • One page. No sign-up, no account, nothing to install.
  • No tracking. Your tasks stay in your browser and are never sent anywhere.
  • "Done" takes a task off the wheel, and a little confetti marks the small win.
  • Light and dark modes that follow your device.

05 · After launch

Shipping is where looking after it starts

  • Moved it to its own home on my portfolio when its old address stopped working.
  • Fixed a bug where the wheel could announce a different task from the one under the pointer.
  • Made every piece of text meet the WCAG AA contrast minimum, in both modes.
  • Kept it working when a browser blocks storage, and made the note say honestly that tasks won't be saved.

06 · What it doesn't do

Deliberate gaps

  • It doesn't rank your tasks or know your deadlines. That judgement stays with you.
  • It doesn't sync between devices.
  • It doesn't collect usage data, so I have no numbers on how it's used. That's a trade-off I chose.
